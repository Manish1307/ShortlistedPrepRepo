How it works

From a spoken question to a private answer in seconds

Here is exactly what happens, and where your information goes.

1. It hears the room

{{name}} listens to the sound of your meeting and, if you choose, your microphone. This stays on your computer.

2. It writes down what was said

An offline speech engine turns each phrase into text, on your PC, in a couple of seconds. The audio is discarded straight afterwards.

3. It spots the questions

When there is a short pause, the new text is checked for questions. Clear one-line questions go straight on; messy or multi-part speech is first tidied up by the AI into clean questions.

4. It writes the answers

Each question is answered by the AI service you chose, using your subject line and a few recent lines of context. Several answers can be written at the same time.

5. You read it, privately

Answers appear in a window that is excluded from screen capture. Click a question on the left to read its answer on the right.

What stays here, and what is sent

✅ Stays on your computer

  • All audio from your meeting and microphone
  • Speech recognition
  • Your AI keys (encrypted by Windows)
  • Your private notes
  • A local activity log you can delete

☁️ Sent to your AI service

  • The text of a detected question
  • Your subject line
  • A few recent lines of class text for context

The service is Google (Gemini) or Anthropic (Claude), depending on your choice. Their terms and privacy policies apply to that text. Free AI plans may use submitted text to improve products; use a paid key if that matters to you.

How the private window works

The assistant window asks Windows to exclude it from screen capture, so it is left out when you share your screen or a window in most meeting apps. We recommend testing with a colleague before your first class, because screen-sharing tools differ.

Please use it responsibly. {{name}} is meant to help teachers and trainers. Follow your school's and platform's rules, and the laws on recording or transcribing calls where you live. Do not use it to cheat in exams or to mislead others. See the Terms of use.
